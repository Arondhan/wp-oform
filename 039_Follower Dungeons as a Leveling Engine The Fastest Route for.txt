    <h1>Follower Dungeons as a Leveling Engine: The Fastest Route for DPS Alts</h1>
    <p>Follower dungeons solved the worst part of leveling a DPS alt: waiting for a tank that never arrives. But the format is not automatically fast. Dungeon choice, follower composition, and buff management decide whether a session flies or crawls. This guide breaks down the methods that actually shorten the climb and the mistakes that quietly add hours.</p>
  </header>

  <section class="data-table">
    <h2>Which strategies actually speed up follower runs?</h2>
    <p><strong>Dungeon selection, follower synergy, and experience boosts are the three levers that matter most, and they do not cost the same.</strong></p>
    <p>Every leveling method has a price in time, resources, or attention. Follower dungeons are attractive because the time cost is low and the resource cost is manageable. The table below ranks the levers by their effect on speed.</p>
    <table>
      <thead>
        <tr>

    <p>Most players overinvest in the third row because it is easy to buy, and underinvest in the second because it requires thought. The best returns come from fixing the cheap levers first.</p>

  <section>
    <h2>How much does dungeon choice matter?</h2>
    <p><strong>Choosing a dungeon with a short layout and high experience per minute matters more than choosing the highest-level option available.</strong></p>
    <p>Experience per hour is a function of clear speed, not dungeon prestige. A slightly lower-level dungeon with a straightforward layout often beats a higher-level dungeon full of detours. Look for short routes, few mandatory bosses, and enemy types your class handles comfortably.</p>
    <p>Community resources and addons can point you toward the current best experience rates, but the final decision should account for your alt's strengths. A dungeon that is efficient for one spec can be miserable for another.</p>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>Why does follower synergy beat raw item level?</h2>
    <p><strong>Followers with complementary abilities clear packs faster than a random assortment of stronger individual units.</strong></p>
    <p>Synergy is about covering weaknesses. If your alt is built for single-target damage, followers that debuff enemies or apply steady healing keep the run moving. If your alt lacks crowd control, a follower that provides it prevents the pulls that stall a dungeon.</p>
    <p>Experiment with combinations instead of accepting the default party. The difference is most visible on packs where your alt would otherwise need to stop and recover. A smooth run is not just faster; it is less mentally taxing, which matters when you are leveling for hours.</p>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>How do you stack experience buffs correctly?</h2>
    <p><strong>Acquire the boosts before you queue, apply them before the first pull, and track their duration so none of them expire mid-run.</strong></p>
    <p>Experience items are straightforward but easy to waste. A buff that expires during a boss fight is a buff you paid for and did not use. The routine is simple:</p>
    <ol>
      <li>Identify which experience boosts are available from vendors, events, or your account's existing unlocks.</li>
      <li>Apply them immediately before entering the dungeon rather than at the start of the session.</li>
      <li>Pair the buff window with your most efficient dungeon, not your most convenient one.</li>
      <li>Watch the timer and refresh between runs instead of discovering the expiry mid-pull.</li>
    </ol>
    <p>Stacking boosts with a well-chosen dungeon is where the biggest time savings appear. Either lever alone helps; together they change the pace of the whole session.</p>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>What should you track while leveling?</h2>
    <p><strong>Track experience per hour, not experience per run, and adjust as soon as the rate drops.</strong></p>
    <p>A run that feels fast can still be inefficient if the downtime between pulls is long. Note how long a full dungeon takes, how much experience it pays, and how much time you lose to travel and recovery. When the rate falls, change one variable at a time: dungeon, follower mix, or buff usage.</p>
    <p>Keeping a simple log for a few sessions is enough to reveal which combination works for your alt. Once you know it, repeat it until the level bracket changes and forces a new plan.</p>
    <p>A simple spreadsheet is enough: run start, run end, experience gained, and any buffs active. After a handful of runs, the pattern becomes obvious, and you can stop guessing which dungeon deserves your next hour. The log also makes it easy to compare a new follower setup against the old one without relying on memory.</p>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>Which mistakes stretch a short session into a long one?</h2>
    <p><strong>Repeating an inefficient dungeon, ignoring follower composition, and running without buffs are the three most common time sinks.</strong></p>
    <p>The pattern is consistent: players find a dungeon they like, run it long after it stops being efficient, and blame the format for the slowdown. Others accept the default followers and never test alternatives. And many simply forget the experience items sitting in their bags.</p>
    <p>Follower dungeons are a tool, not a strategy. The players who level fastest are the ones who treat each session as a small optimization problem and fix the cheapest variable first.</p>
  </section>
